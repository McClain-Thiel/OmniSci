# Experimental design

Write the primary hypothesis and estimand before choosing measurements. Define
the experimental unit, biological and technical replicates, primary outcome,
decision threshold, and what result would falsify or materially weaken the
hypothesis.

Specify positive, negative, vehicle, batch, and process controls as applicable.
Identify confounders and decide how randomization, blocking, matching, blinding,
or covariate adjustment will address them.

Base sample-size reasoning on the smallest effect worth detecting, expected
variance, desired power, allocation, and planned analysis. Label heuristic
sample sizes as heuristics.

Predefine exclusions, stopping rules, missing-data handling, multiplicity, and
the analysis plan. Include a pilot when feasibility or variance is too uncertain
for a defensible full design.

End with a reproducibility checklist: materials and identifiers, protocol
version, instrument settings, software/environment, raw-data location, metadata,
and the artifacts that constitute a successful run.
